About AquaGuard AI

Understanding the system, its purpose, and its limitations.

Mission & SDG Alignment

AquaGuard AI is an educational and awareness tool built around UN Sustainable Development Goal 14: Life Below Water. It demonstrates how accessible, open-source image-processing techniques can be applied to generate preliminary, experimental risk flags for water body health — helping communities and researchers quickly triage areas that may warrant formal laboratory investigation.

It is not a certified environmental monitoring instrument. Results must always be validated by certified water-quality testing.

Technology Stack
  • Backend: Python Flask
  • Image Analysis: OpenCV + NumPy
  • Database: MongoDB Atlas (free tier)
  • Maps: Leaflet.js + OpenStreetMap
  • Charts: Chart.js
  • Frontend: Bootstrap 5 + Vanilla JS
  • Deployment: Gunicorn + .env config
  • License: MIT (open source)
Important Limitations
  • Analysis is based on visible-spectrum pixel data only. Chemical and biological pollutants that do not alter visible colour or texture cannot be detected.
  • Lighting conditions, time of day, weather, and camera quality significantly affect results.
  • The system cannot detect dissolved pollutants, heavy metals, pathogens, or micro-plastics.
  • A "Low" risk result does not mean the water is safe.
  • A "High" risk result does not confirm actual pollution — always verify with lab testing.
Analysis Methodology

Four independent sub-analyses are combined into a weighted composite score (0–100):

  1. Colour Anomaly (35%) – Pixels outside typical blue/blue-green water hues (brownish, red, yellow) raise the score.
  2. Turbidity Proxy (25%) – High brightness variance in the Lab colour space suggests suspended particles.
  3. Algae Bloom Indicator (20%) – Green-channel dominance over blue signals possible algae or eutrophication.
  4. Dark Patch Detection (20%) – Very dark, low-saturation regions may indicate oil sheens or dense matter.

All weights and thresholds are explicitly documented in analyzer.py.
